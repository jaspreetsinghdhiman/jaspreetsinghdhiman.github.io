A RESEARCH-LED COLLECTION

Understanding the living heritage.

Markets are more than places to buy and sell. They connect farms to towns, makers to customers, local materials to skilled labour and neighbourhoods to wider trade networks. Punjab’s market heritage includes weekly rural gatherings, grain mandis, urban bazaars, craft clusters and cross-regional exchange.

How to read this collection

These are editorial introductions, not claims that every practice is identical across Punjab. Regional names, dates and customs vary. Individual records should be supported by reliable references, local knowledge and community review.

Punjabi terms are shown in Gurmukhi where appropriate. Shahmukhi and regional vocabulary can be added through reviewed contributions.

01

Village haats and weekly markets ↗

Weekly markets bring together produce, household goods, tools, clothing, livestock-related services and social exchange. Their rhythm depends on local calendars and transport. Each record should identify the market day, locality, goods traded and changes remembered by vendors.

02

Grain mandis and agricultural exchange ↗

Agricultural markets link cultivation with storage, transport, pricing and procurement. Their modern systems are shaped by regulation and policy as well as longstanding networks of commission agents, traders, labourers and farmers.

03

Village haats and weekly markets ↗

Historic bazaars in cities such as Amritsar, Lahore, Patiala and Multan contain distinct commercial and craft histories. Street names, building fabric, shop specialisms and community memories should be documented block by block, not treated as interchangeable.

04

Craftspeople, workshops and livelihoods ↗

Metalworkers, weavers, embroiderers, potters, carpenters, leather workers and other makers often depend on local markets and family knowledge. Record the maker’s own account, tools, materials, training and current challenges with permission.
