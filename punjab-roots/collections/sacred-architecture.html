A RESEARCH-LED COLLECTION

Understanding the living heritage.

Punjab’s built heritage includes gurdwaras, mosques, Sufi shrines, temples, samadhs, deras and other places of worship. Their architecture reflects patronage, craft, local materials, changing communities and centuries of repair. A respectful archive records each site on its own terms.

How to read this collection

These are editorial introductions, not claims that every practice is identical across Punjab. Regional names, dates and customs vary. Individual records should be supported by reliable references, local knowledge and community review.

Punjabi terms are shown in Gurmukhi where appropriate. Shahmukhi and regional vocabulary can be added through reviewed contributions.

01

Gurdwara architecture and living practice ↗

Gurdwaras are spaces of sangat, kirtan, learning, remembrance and langar. Architectural features can include a prayer hall, sanctum, domes, gateways, sarovar and community facilities, but no single layout describes every site. Records should distinguish historic fabric from later additions.

02

Mosques, dargahs and Sufi landscapes ↗

Mosques and Sufi shrines across Punjab reflect varied regional histories, patronage and craft traditions. Tilework, brickwork, calligraphy, courtyards, domes and minarets should be documented with precise site identification and specialist sources.

FULL ARTICLE
03

Temples, deras and neighbourhood shrines ↗

Hindu temples, deras, samadhs and smaller local shrines form part of Punjab’s diverse religious landscape. Their histories are often preserved through local custodians, inscriptions, community records and oral testimony.

FULL ARTICLE
04

How to document a sacred site ↗

Capture the official and local name, village or city, district, coordinates, faith/community context where publicly documented, construction and renovation dates, architect or patron when verified, materials, access guidance and photography permissions. Never photograph worshippers or restricted interiors without consent.
