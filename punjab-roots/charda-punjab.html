A GROWING, SOURCE-LED COLLECTION

Many places. Many voices.

This page focuses on the present-day Indian state of Punjab while recognising that many cultural forms have histories extending across the wider Punjab region. Regional and community differences matter: Majha, Doaba and Malwa should not be treated as interchangeable.

01 · CRAFT · TEXTILES

Phulkari — stories in every stitch

Phulkari embroidery uses counted darning stitches and silk thread on handwoven cotton khaddar. Bagh forms can cover the cloth densely, while other styles use more open patterns. It has carried ceremonial, familial and everyday meanings, with designs and practices changing across generations.

Incredible India · Phulkari craft ↗

02 · MUSIC · PERFORMANCE

Bhangra, Giddha & folk sound

Punjab’s cultural life includes energetic Bhangra and Jhumar traditions, women’s Giddha and boliyan, devotional music and regional folk instruments. Performance styles vary by occasion and locality; the archive will document performers and community context rather than reduce them to stage stereotypes.

Government of Punjab · Culture ↗

03 · VILLAGE LIFE · OBJECTS

Homes, tools & rural knowledge

A village home can hold generations of practical knowledge: grain storage, hand mills, spinning tools, cooking vessels, woven furnishings and agricultural implements. The Rural Museum in Ludhiana preserves examples of domestic objects, instruments, clothing and village settings.

Incredible India · Rural Museum, Ludhiana ↗

04 · FOODWAYS · SEASONS

Fields, kitchens & shared meals

Food traditions are shaped by season, household, district and community. Makki di roti and sarson da saag are well-known examples, alongside dairy, pulses, breads and local preparations. We will record recipes with family attribution and avoid presenting one menu as universal to Punjab.

Government of Punjab · Culture and cuisine ↗

05 · PLACES · MEMORY

Majha, Doaba & Malwa

The three broad cultural-geographic regions are often used to describe different parts of Indian Punjab. Their towns, rivers, dialects, craft practices and migration histories deserve more detailed district-level records instead of a single generic regional story.
