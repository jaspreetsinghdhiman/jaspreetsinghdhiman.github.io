A GROWING, SOURCE-LED COLLECTION

Many places. Many voices.

Sanjha Virsa does not mean that every tradition is identical or that Partition erased distinct identities. It is a way to document connections alongside difference, displacement and the separate histories that followed 1947.

01 · HISTORY · PLACE

A region before the border

Before 1947, Punjab was a connected historical region with cities, villages, markets, shrines, farms and routes that later fell on different sides of an international border. Records should name the historical place and the present-day country separately, where known.

Explore our Partition memory feature ↗

02 · 1947 · DISPLACEMENT

Remembering Partition through people

The 1947 division brought mass displacement, violence, loss and the rebuilding of lives across new borders. Family trunks, utensils, letters, photographs and keepsakes can hold histories that official timelines cannot capture alone. These memories must be handled with consent and care.

Explore our Partition memory feature ↗

03 · ORAL HISTORY · MEMORY

Listen before telling

Oral histories preserve the voices of witnesses and descendants: memories of home, migration, languages, work, food, friendship and return. The 1947 Partition Archive documents witness accounts and personal materials from communities affected across the region and diaspora.

Explore oral histories and family memory ↗

04 · CRAFT · CULTURAL CONTINUITY

Traditions that cross borders

Crafts, songs, embroidery, storytelling and foodways can have histories that predate modern borders and continue in changed forms across communities. Phulkari is one example whose documented regional history spans the Punjab region now divided between India and Pakistan. Specific forms and makers still need precise attribution.

Explore Phulkari and regional craft traditions ↗

05 · LANGUAGE · MANY IDENTITIES

Punjabi is not one voice

Punjabi is spoken and written in multiple varieties and scripts, and the region is home to other distinct language communities too. A shared archive should preserve Gurmukhi and Shahmukhi materials, local speech, names and self-identification without flattening differences.
